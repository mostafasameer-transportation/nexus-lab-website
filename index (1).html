About the Lab

Five ways to look at the same bridge.

Nexus Lab is a civil engineering research group working across structures, transportation, and the cities they serve — from Dhaka to Miami. Our name is also our method: five connected lines of inquiry that treat infrastructure as one continuous system, not five separate problems.

Research Focus

What NEXUS stands for, in practice.

Each pillar is a real line of active work in the lab — not a category we made up for the acronym.

N
Networks

Transportation network resilience

Modeling mobility networks under stress — from V2X-enabled e-scooter and mixed-traffic simulations in CARLA and SUMO, to folding network efficiency directly into how bridge repairs get prioritized.

E
Engineering

Structural & seismic engineering

Nonlinear finite element analysis in Abaqus, CFRP/FRP strengthening of reinforced concrete beam-column joints under cyclic loading, and QA/QC on live infrastructure megaprojects.

X
eXploration

Multi-hazard risk & digital twins

GIS-based coastal exposure and flood-risk workflows spanning multiple U.S. states, hurricane-driven rain intrusion studies, and digital twin frameworks that fuse hazard data with building performance.

U
Urban Systems

Equity-focused infrastructure

Decision-support frameworks that link hazard intensity, structural condition, and community accessibility — plus research into socioeconomic mobility and post-pandemic urban travel patterns.

S
Structures

Bridge rehabilitation prioritization

Mining multi-state National Bridge Inventory data to build interpretable frameworks for deciding what gets rehabilitated first — and defending why.

Who's in the Lab

Two countries, one lab.

Led from Florida International University, with researchers based in Dhaka, Bangladesh — Nexus Lab is small by design and collaborative by necessity.

Mostafa Sameer, M.Sc.
HEAD INVESTIGATOR & PRINCIPAL INVESTIGATOR
Ph.D. Candidate, Civil Engineering — Florida International University

Mostafa leads Nexus Lab's work on bridge infrastructure analytics and rehabilitation prioritization, drawing on multi-state National Bridge Inventory data from Florida, Texas, Pennsylvania, California, and New York. His research also spans GIS-based coastal exposure and flood-risk modeling, multi-hazard analysis, and digital twin frameworks for resilient infrastructure decisions. He holds an M.Sc. in Transportation Engineering from Wayne State University and a B.Sc. in Civil Engineering from Ahsanullah University of Science & Technology (AUST).
